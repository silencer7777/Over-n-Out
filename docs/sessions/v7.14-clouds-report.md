## What the sky was before (code terms)

A single gradient dome (canvas gradient from the lighting
profile) + sun disc/glow + a dusk star field. **There were no
clouds anywhere in the game** — no cloud geometry, texture,
or shader existed. The far environment above the V7.9 ridge
rings was an empty gradient.

## What was built

- **`makeCloudTexture(THREE, p)` (js/lighting.js):** a
  procedural 1024×256 transparent canvas per lighting
  profile — clusters of layered radial-gradient puffs
  (5–9 overlapping soft ellipses per cluster; the layering
  and the flat-bottomed cluster spread are what make them
  read as clouds rather than blobs), tinted by the profile,
  density by the profile's new `clouds.cover`, seeded so a
  map's sky is identical every load, drawn triple-wide so
  the horizontal wrap is seamless, with a destination-in
  vertical fade feathering both edges to nothing.
- **The band (js/world.js):** ONE open cylinder (radius 285,
  just inside the 300 dome; height 120, centered at y=95 so
  clouds start ~7° above the horizon and the V7.9 ridges /
  tree-line silhouettes stay readable), BackSide,
  depthWrite off, renderOrder −9 (right after the dome),
  named 'cloud-band'. No colliders; castShadow false.
- **Per-map character (profile `clouds` rows +
  applyLightingProfile re-paints the band like the dome):**
  Compound — warm-white puffs (tint 0xfff6ea, cover 0.8,
  alpha 0.85); Forest — bright white, cover 0.9; Snow —
  pale flat overcast sheet (0xe9eff4, cover 1.0, alpha 0.75);
  Mars — thin rusty dust haze (0xdca26a, cover 0.35,
  alpha 0.38): four skies stay four skies.
- **Drift (js/main.js):** the band rotates 0.004 rad/s from
  both ticks — clouds cross the sky like weather, ~26 min
  per revolution, no per-frame allocation.
